How much do hearing aids cost in Nashville?

People often hear that hearing aids cost $3,000 to $8,000, and sometimes more. That number is usually a bundled price. Most hearing clinics typically pay $1,100 to $2,500 per hearing aid. Then the costs of the provider's time and expertise, business expenses, and follow-up care are bundled into your total price, without a clear breakdown of what you are actually paying for.

NHCC uses an unbundled service model. You will always know the cost of the device and the cost of services. Each service is itemized so you can see exactly what you are paying for. We do not post a single "starting at" price here, because the right device and the services you need depend on your hearing, lifestyle, and budget. After your evaluation, Dr. Angley will walk through options with clear numbers.

Financing through Cherry and CareCredit is available to help make hearing care accessible across a range of budgets, and insurance may apply for qualifying policies. See patient information for payment details, or read more about our pricing approach on the About page.

Prescription hearing aids vs. over-the-counter (OTC)

As of August 17, 2022, the FDA released regulations for over-the-counter hearing aids. OTC devices are meant for adults with a perceived mild to moderate degree of hearing loss. They are self-fit and sold without a hearing test. They can be a reasonable entry point for the right person, and Nashville's Hearing & Communication Center offers OTC devices to patients.

Prescription hearing aids are programmed by a licensed audiologist based on a full diagnostic evaluation. They can address a wider range of hearing loss and include professional fitting, real-ear measurement, and follow-up care. A hearing test should come first, so you know whether OTC, a prescription device, or another option such as an assistive listening device is the better fit.

If hearing loss is sudden, much worse in one ear, or comes with drainage, dizziness, ear pain, or tinnitus in only one ear, skip self-fitting an OTC device and get a professional evaluation first. Our longer guide is here: OTC vs. prescriptive hearing aids.

Real-ear measurement: why the fitting matters

A hearing aid fitting is about more than how a device sits in your ear. Real-ear measurement, also called hearing aid verification or probe microphone measurement, is how Dr. Angley confirms that the aid is providing the correct amount of amplification and meeting your hearing prescription.

The process starts with placing a small, flexible microphone in the ear canal, then placing the hearing aid in the ear. That microphone measures how loud the hearing aid is in your ear. While it records the sound, acoustics, and shape of your ear, settings can be changed in real time.

Uncalibrated hearing aids may be better than none at all, but to get the full benefit of the device, it needs to be calibrated to your specific ear and hearing loss. A drugstore pair of readers can improve vision; they are not the same as prescription glasses. Real-ear measurement is the hearing-care version of that difference, and it is associated with higher patient satisfaction.

During a typical fitting, which usually lasts about 45 to 60 minutes, you will also get any final adjustments and instruction on insertion, removal, and everyday care, including how to change or charge batteries. Learn more about the full care path on our services and hearing testing pages.

Follow-up care, the trial period, and help with aids you already own

Devices purchased from us include a minimum 30-day trial period. Return appointments reassess your goals, adjust settings, and troubleshoot. Telehealth remote care is available for supported devices, and concierge services can include in-home assessments, device setup, and troubleshooting when that is the better option.

If you already have hearing aids, including devices purchased elsewhere, you can bring them in. NHCC provides third-party maintenance and repair: cleaning, checking, recalibration, and support for hearing aids of any brand.

Three common reasons people are unhappy with hearing aids are that the devices were never verified with real-ear measurement, they no longer match a change in hearing, or they were not the right solution for that person's hearing profile and lifestyle. We can evaluate which of those applies and what to do next.

Hearing aids and tinnitus

Tinnitus (ringing, buzzing, or hissing in the ears) is common, and treatable. We can help you understand what is driving your symptoms and build a plan to reduce tinnitus-related stress and improve quality of life. When hearing loss is also present, a properly fitted hearing aid may be part of that plan, along with counseling and other management options. Read more on our tinnitus care section.

Frequently asked questions

Why are hearing aids so expensive?

Most hearing clinics typically pay $1,100 to $2,500 per hearing aid. Then the costs of the provider's time and expertise, business expenses, and related care are bundled into your total price, often without a clear view of what you are paying for. At NHCC, device cost and service cost are itemized separately.

Are real-ear measurements really necessary?

Yes, if you want the device calibrated to your ear and hearing loss. Real-ear measurement is an evidence-based practice that results in much higher patient satisfaction with hearing aids.

Are OTC hearing aids enough?

They can be, for some adults with perceived mild to moderate hearing loss and no medical warning signs. They are not a substitute for a diagnostic hearing test, and they are not the right first step when red-flag symptoms are present. NHCC can help you compare OTC and prescription options after an evaluation.

Do hearing aids help tinnitus?

Tinnitus is treatable, and a hearing aid can be part of a management plan when hearing loss is also present. The first step is an evaluation so the plan fits your symptoms, not a one-size-fits-all device setting.
